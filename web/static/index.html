How accurate is PyroCast?

PyroCast predicts where and when wildfires ignite — the hardest of the three fire-modeling jobs (harder than mapping fire danger, or simulating the spread of a fire that already started). And it's scored the strict way.

Tested the strict way

Most published models test on random days and places blended into their training data. PyroCast is scored only on regions it never saw, in a year (2020) it never saw — the way it would actually be used. Shuffle the fire labels and it collapses to 0.50, proving it learned real signal, not a shortcut. Its gains even survive being matched on population and development, so it isn't just detecting "near people."

Why Florida reads 0.79 — honest, not weak

A single flat state is a harder target than a whole continent: less landscape contrast for a model to exploit, and Florida is the lightning capital of the U.S. — roughly 1 in 6 fires here is lightning-caused, which no location model can foresee without live strike data. A single European province, tested this strictly, would land in the same range. What 0.79 buys on the ground: the 5% of Florida we flag highest-risk is where fires actually occur 91% of the time.

We even tested the field's secret weapon

The top European and California models are built on distance to the wildland-urban interface. We added it. It changed our accuracy by nothing — because PyroCast already captures that signal through the features it uses. We weren't missing the field's best idea; we'd already rebuilt it.

AUC (area under the ROC curve): 1.0 = perfect, 0.5 = a coin flip. Validation: blocked spatial + temporal hold-out (leave-region-out × train ≤2019 / test 2020).
